The main purpose of the meeting was to explain *how to give and receive effective peer feedback*, especially for the current project and SRS-related work.

The lecturer explained that feedback is *not criticism*. It is a way to help improve someone’s work. This is especially important in the IT industry, where professionals regularly use feedback during code reviews, design reviews, project reviews, and even when reviewing AI-generated outputs. 

*Why Receiving Feedback Is Important*

Receiving feedback helps you:

* improve the quality of your work
* identify weaknesses or blind spots
* learn from mistakes
* discover alternative solutions
* prepare for professional industry work

Feedback can show problems that you may not notice by yourself. 

 *Why Giving Feedback Is Important*

Giving feedback also helps the person providing it.

It improves:

* critical thinking
* analytical thinking
* professional communication
* teamwork
* collaboration
* ability to evaluate projects

By reviewing another student’s work, you may also notice ideas that can improve your own project. 

*Important Rule*

The lecturer emphasized:

> *Feedback should focus on improving the work, not criticizing the person.*

For example:

Bad feedback:

> “Your interface is confusing.”

Better feedback:

> “The navigation on the homepage is difficult to understand. Consider using clearer menu labels.”

The better version is specific and also gives a possible improvement. 

*The 3C Rule for Good Feedback*

Good feedback should follow the *3Cs*:

 *1. Clear*
Explain the issue clearly and specifically.

*2. Constructive*
Do not only identify the problem. Suggest how it could be improved.

 *3. Courteous*
Use respectful and professional language.

A simple way to remember it is:

*Clear + Constructive + Courteous*

 *SBI Feedback Format*

The lecturer introduced the *SBI framework* to structure feedback.

 *S – Situation*
Where or when did the issue happen?

*B – Behaviour*
What exactly did you observe?

 *I – Impact*
Why does it matter? What effect can it have?

You can also add a:

 *Suggestion*
How could the work be improved?



 *SBI Example*

*Situation:* During the system demo,

*Behaviour:* the login page takes a long time to load.

*Impact:* users may become frustrated.

*Suggestion:* optimize the database query or reduce unnecessary graphics.


 *Is SBI Mandatory?*
No.

During the Q&A, the lecturer clearly explained that *SBI is not a strict compulsory format*.
However, using SBI can help you organize your feedback properly and make it more useful.
You also do not need to force every single sentence into SBI format. 

So the safest approach is:

*Use SBI + a suggestion whenever possible.*

*How to Receive Feedback Properly*

When someone gives you feedback:
1. Listen carefully.
2. Do not interrupt.
3. Ask clarification questions if something is unclear.
4. Thank the person who gave the feedback.
5. Analyze the feedback.
6. Decide how to improve your work.
